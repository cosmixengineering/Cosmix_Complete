# Attendance payroll inputs

New schema-3 ordinary and late deductions require a two-decimal rounding mode (explicit half-up
or half-even). Whole-currency input is rejected with `deduction_precision_required`, even when
the late rule is disabled. Legacy schema 1/2 interpretation and separate whole-rupee OT remain
unchanged.

The restricted financial producer calculates proposed ordinary deductions and late-threshold candidates from verified attendance snapshots. It does not change compensation, stop salary, post payroll, pay anyone, or mark daily summaries payroll-ready. The financial service retains an immutable encrypted employee/month revision with the complete source manifest; recomputation appends a revision, never adds different revisions together.

Schema 3 explicitly supplies ordinary and late financial rules. Ordinary absence uses the configured full unpaid-day fraction, unpaid half-day uses its configured fraction, and hourly shortfall uses basic monthly salary divided by the configured month-day divisor and configured scheduled hours. An approved unpaid leave interval totaling exactly half of scheduled paid capacity also uses the half-day fraction when the remaining duty/paid leave is fully credited; this does not require a late-arrival half-day classification. Other partial leave or additional shortfall retains hourly treatment, with no interval rounding into half-day eligibility. Paid leave, weekly off and applicable holidays are exempt. A half-day containing approved paid leave is capped to its remaining unpaid capacity. The explicitly configured daily unpaid break reduces missing duty once and is not itself charged as absence. No pre-shift credit is invented. Approved overtime remains separate and is excluded from ordinary time by the verified daily producer.

Late thresholds retain each historical day's own classification. Their counting signature contains only normalized `LateDeduction` settings, including enablement, period, threshold, amount kind and overlap treatment. New policy-version identity, salary change, ordinary settings or arrival settings do not reset the count. A change to late settings does. Month, calendar year, July–June fiscal year and an explicitly anchored week are supported. Disabled late rules produce no late charge. The completing day's protected compensation sets the salary, and the completing payroll month supplies the actual-calendar-month divisor. Configured charges can use a day fraction, fixed PKR or scheduled-hour rate.

Each completed group has one candidate late charge. Ordinary charges for its exact occurrence dates remain visible; the effective late candidate is `max(0, late candidate − ordinary candidates)`, so the combined amount is the larger of the two. A suppressed late candidate remains visible with zero effective amount and an application code. Cross-month ordinary deductions require reconciliation with protected earlier financial revisions; the preview retains the candidates but reports `prior_deduction_reconciliation_required`. It does not assume an earlier deduction was settled. Legal applicability and payroll activation are separate requirements; a calculated candidate is not a lawful or approved penalty.

Salary, fractions and ticks remain rational values until final configured currency rounding. Lines retain numerator/denominator, full candidate and effective amount, salary/rate source, compensation version and digest, dated policy versions, daily source fingerprints and source IDs. Full manifests preserve the daily policy, roster, raw/assessment/correction/leave/OT evidence and historical authorization scopes. General daily summaries receive no financial payload.

The caller must authorize `ResourceScopesAsync` before `CalculateAsync` in the same authorization transaction. The source range is derived from the target month's dated policy metadata: monthly-only settings use the month, weekly settings include the preceding partial week, and annual/fiscal settings include their period start. It contains no more than 365/366 days and is clamped to verified employment coverage. Lifecycle and joined/departure provenance reuse the month-coverage rules; unexpected raw/session/summary dates outside verified employment remain a conflict. The repository batches lifecycle, canonical session revisions and compensation history; protected compensation is read once per used version per calculation. Production limits are 366 source days, 2,048 lifecycle records, 32,768 session revisions and 4,096 compensation versions, with overflow rejected. Daily policy/roster/leave/raw/correction/OT provenance remains a bounded sequential call per covered day to reuse the authoritative guards; this is not an unbounded employee scan. Compensation approval and decision collections are fetched in batches.

Protected compensation validation compares approved change proposals and decision `before/after` payloads to the exact immutable record. Initial compensation must match the approved hiring snapshot and onboarding decision. Unsupported wage basis/currency, missing policy/time/salary, pending leave or stale/corrupt attendance evidence remain unavailable. Only monthly PKR is supported. Carry-forward valuation, overtime settlement, deductions from previously finalized payroll and payment release are not inferred here.

`VerifyPayrollInputs.cs` is a standalone synthetic harness for pure rules and the actual producer with protected compensation fixtures. It does not create a test project, contact SQL/network, or write employee data. Run it using the repository's offline standalone-harness command after obtaining the shared build slot.

Executed verification for this implementation: 49 payroll-input pure/real-producer assertions passed, including initial-hiring salary provenance and configurable unpaid half-leave cases; 6 production payroll-input SQL translation assertions passed with zero SQL connections; and all 67 existing daily-calculation assertions passed. No test project, SQL connection, network call or employee/compensation mutation was used.
